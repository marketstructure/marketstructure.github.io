  • Hyperscale lease anchors 1GW Tokyo data centre campus. Goodman Group has signed a 20‑year lease with an unnamed global hyperscale client for the first 50‑megawatt phase of its Tsukuba Tech Central data centre campus in Greater Tokyo, part of a planned 1‑gigawatt development. The facility is under construction and expected to enter service in early 2028, with Goodman to develop, own, fit out and operate the building, supported by power secured from Tokyo Electric Power Company and multiple dedicated dark‑fibre routes to Tokyo’s main interconnection points.capitalbrief+4

  • Vietnam advances sweeping land acquisition reforms. Vietnam’s government has proposed allowing land acquisition and construction on development sites to commence before compensation and resettlement arrangements are fully finalised, aiming to remove “institutional bottlenecks” and accelerate housing and infrastructure delivery. Draft rules would give affected residents priority access to new residential land or housing in the same area once projects proceed and introduce more transparent, data‑driven land pricing and clearer criteria for eligible projects. The proposals are being tabled to the National Assembly in an extraordinary August session, with approval targeted later this year.theinvestor+3

  • Bain Capital exits Embassy Office Parks REIT. On 17 August, Bain Capital, via affiliate APAC Company XXIII Ltd, sold its entire 5.64 percent stake in Embassy Office Parks REIT, India’s first listed REIT, through open‑market block transactions. The firm offloaded about 53.45 million units at roughly ₹435 per unit, for total proceeds of around ₹2,325 crore. Management has indicated that Embassy REIT is pursuing acquisitions of around 10–12 million square feet of additional office space over the next three to four years, after reporting a 17 percent year‑on‑year rise in net operating income in the June quarter.kotakneo+4

  • Modernland seeks second restructuring of US dollar bond. Indonesia’s PT Modernland Realty has filed for a second restructuring of its US$274.5 million dollar bond, according to a 14 August filing, underscoring ongoing liquidity pressures at the residential and industrial township developer. The proposed scheme would extend the bond’s maturity to 30 June 2028, capitalise accrued interest, and apply 3 percent payment‑in‑kind interest from the restructuring effective date, while transferring certain collateral assets into a creditor‑controlled vehicle and offering holders an upfront fee per US$1,000 of principal.
