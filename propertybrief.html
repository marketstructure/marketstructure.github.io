  • China developer and property manager earnings season underscores divergence. Multiple mainland developers and property managers reported H1 2026 results in recent days. Poly Developments’ net profit fell 40.8% year-on-year to RMB19.59 billion on revenues down 11.98%. Greentown China’s revenue dropped 26% to RMB39.48 billion and profit fell 61% to RMB81.7 million.   • Singapore's One Raffles Place nears record office sale. CapitaLand Investment and Malaysian developer IOI Properties Group are close to finalizing their joint-venture acquisition of One Raffles Place, the landmark CBD complex comprising two office towers (62 and 38 storeys) and a retail mall. The deal is valued at just under S$2.4 billion (US$1.9 billion), down from an initial ask of S$2.5 billion, with the stake priced against an OUE REIT-linked valuation of S$2.37 billion at end-2025. Both parties are reportedly weighing partial redevelopment, while UOB, a current occupant, is expected to retain its space post-transaction. 
  • Hong Kong noncore office bargain-hunting continues amid steep discounts. Owner-occupiers are buying into Hong Kong's noncore office market at deep discounts, exemplified by the Estate Agents Authority's purchase of a 7,343 sq ft unit at Wan Chai's OTB Building for HK$70 million (HK$9,533 per sq ft), agreed earlier in August and reported August 21. The price sits below the HK$10,000 psf threshold, though only modestly under the building's 2019 comparable at HK$11,839 psf. Noncore office values remain down more than half from their 2018 peak, with vacancy near 30% in some noncore districts and continued price-cutting by developers and distressed sellers, per Centaline Commercial. This contrasts with Hong Kong's Central Grade A submarket, where JLL separately reported vacancy falling to a 31-month low of 12.8% overall (8.0% in Central) as of end-July, though that data point predates the 6-day window and is provided as context only, not as a standalone qualifying item.
  • Northern Metropolis pilot land tender awarded to six-firm consortium. On 24 August 2026, Hong Kong’s Development Bureau awarded the Hung Shui Kiu/Ha Tsuen pilot area in the Northern Metropolis to HSK New Development Limited for HK$1.03 billion under the large-scale land disposal scheme. The consortium includes China Overseas Land & Investment, Sino Land, China Merchants Land, China Resources Land (Overseas), CTG Investment Management and JD.com. The joint venture plans total investment of about HK$16.8 billion (US$2.14 billion) in the pilot development area bordering Shenzhen.
  • Colliers report shows India dominating Asia Pacific office leasing in H1 2026. Colliers’ “Asia Pacific Office Market Insights H1 2026” report, released in late August, finds office leasing across 11 major APAC markets rose 3% year-on-year to 4.6 million sq m (49.5 million sq ft) in the first half. India accounted for more than 70% of total leasing volume, supported by Grade A demand and global capability centres. India, mainland China and Japan together contributed over 95% of regional leasing, underscoring the current concentration of office demand in a few large markets.
  • South Korea moves to revise recently announced property tax overhaul. South Korea’s ruling Democratic Party and government agreed to revisit a comprehensive real estate tax reform unveiled on 3 August, following public backlash. The original plan would have lowered the basic deduction for non-resident single-home owners from 1.2 billion won to 900 million won and raised the annual tax burden cap from 150% to 200%. Authorities are now expected to restore a unified basic deduction and ease the proposed increases in tax burden caps for these owners.
  • Singapore allocates 200MW new data centre capacity to four operators. Singapore’s Economic Development Board and Infocomm Media Development Authority provisionally allocated 200MW of new data centre capacity under the second Data Centre Call for Application. Digital Realty, Equinix, ST Telemedia Global Data Centres and Keppel Data Centres each received 50MW for projects concentrated on Jurong Island. The awards link expansion to high energy-efficiency and significant use of green power, positioning the new facilities as AI-focused, high-density infrastructure within the city-state’s constrained power and land environment.
