Asia Property Briefing – October 8, 2026

This briefing is generated by an AI-augmented workflow with some light review. Please verify critical information independently.

  • Kowloon Land Sale Hits Five-Year High — Kerry Properties, through subsidiary Asia Insight Investments, won the tender for Kowloon Inland Lot No. 11301 at Fat Kwong Street, Ho Man Tin on October 6, paying HK$4.31 billion (US$549 million) on a 50-year land grant. The price equates to HK$20,738 per square foot of gross floor area on the roughly 208,000 sq ft site—38 per cent above the top of market estimates of HK$9,000–15,000 psf—and marks the highest Kowloon residential land price since Lai Sun's October 2021 Broadcast Drive purchase. The tender drew 10 bids.

  • New World Development Debt Talks — Bain Capital is exploring an investment in New World Development as the developer seeks to reduce its heavy debt burden, with a potential structure also involving a fresh capital injection by the controlling Cheng family, Bloomberg reported October 2. New World's total debt stood at about HK$143.3 billion (US$18 billion) at end-June, including HK$23.2 billion due within one year and HK$41.7 billion due in one to two years. Discussions remain ongoing with no certainty of a transaction.

  • Hong Kong's First Listed Property Debt Fund — Gaw Capital filed a preliminary prospectus on October 2 for the Gaw Capital Real Estate Private Debt OFC, the first real estate private debt fund to seek a Hong Kong main board listing. The closed-end vehicle will allocate at least 90 per cent of assets to property-related debt including senior and construction loans, may borrow up to 30 per cent of net asset value, and targets quarterly distributions. It has identified a HK$2.29 billion pipeline across Hong Kong, Australia, South Korea, Thailand and the Maldives. Jefferies is sole sponsor.

  • Japanese Corporate Real Estate Monetisation — Blackstone, Bain Capital, Warburg Pincus and BGO are preparing final bids due end-October for Fuji Media's Sankei Building unit, expected to be valued around ¥1 trillion (US$6.3 billion) including debt, in one of Japan's largest real estate takeovers. Separately, Japan Post established Japan Post Real Estate Investment Management on October 1 with ¥300 million capital to acquire properties from the group's ¥1.6 trillion (US$10.1 billion) portfolio—roughly ¥700 billion above book value—for private funds, with operations targeted between October 2027 and March 2028.

  • One Raffles Place Nears S$2.4 Billion Sale — IOI Properties Group and CapitaLand Investment have entered exclusive due diligence on a 50-50 joint bid for One Raffles Place, submitted to owners OUE Reit and UOB, with indicative pricing in the S$2.3–2.4 billion range. IOI chief executive Lee Yeow Seng said on October 7 that final sales and purchase terms are under negotiation, with the JV structure incorporating tag-along and exit flexibility. OUE Reit's controlled firm holds 81.54 per cent; UOB holds 18.46 per cent.

  • Brookfield Enters India Logistics — Brookfield announced on October 7 its acquisition of 100 per cent interest in eight Grade A industrial and logistics parks from ESR India, committing ₹4,300 crore (approximately US$444–450 million) toward the purchase and future development—its first warehousing-focused transaction in India. The 10.5 million sq ft portfolio spans around 400 acres across Mumbai, Pune, Delhi-NCR, Chennai and Kolkata, is roughly 98 per cent leased, and will continue to be managed by ESR in the near term.
