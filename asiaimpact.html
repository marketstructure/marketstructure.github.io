  • China's first active ETFs sell out at the cap. The first 18 actively managed ETFs, each capped at RMB2bn, opened for subscription on 23 October, and by 27 October seven of the nine Shanghai-listed products had reached their cap and the nine had raised RMB16.33bn from close to 100,000 investors, more than a fifth of them institutions. Thirteen of the 18 had closed by 29 October, and a full subscription across the batch would raise RMB36bn. JPMorgan's fund filled on the first day and moved to pro-rata allocation, and its shareholder said before the sale that it would invest US$75m, about RMB500m, of its own capital through JPMorgan Asset Management (Asia Pacific). Because demand exceeded the cap, the RMB2bn limit rather than investor appetite set each product's size, and own-capital seeding is how a foreign house without a domestic active record reached scale in the format.
  • Japanese insurers are securing external asset-management platforms. Meiji Yasuda will raise commitments to Man Group strategies to ¥200 billion, or approximately US$1.25 billion, over three years, mainly in private credit; the firms will co-develop Japanese products, and the insurer may acquire roughly 5% of Man. Dai-ichi Life’s Australian arm, meanwhile, had its A$11.80-a-share proposal for Challenger’s remaining 79.5% rejected, but negotiations remain open. The common mechanism is strategic ownership allied with distribution and investment capability, rather than stand-alone outsourced mandates.
  • Korean financial groups are building operating leverage in Japan. Mirae Asset agreed to acquire 100% of Okasan’s Sanko Securities, securing a local licence and a base for proprietary trading, retail expansion and potential Global X product distribution through Okasan. Samsung Life, Samsung Fire, Samsung Securities and Samsung Asset Management are also establishing a joint Tokyo office focused on market intelligence, alternatives and M&A. The corridor is moving from representative coverage towards licensed infrastructure and coordinated group-level origination.
  • Korean LPs are repricing alternatives around defence. Leading pension, sovereign and insurance investors are reworking portfolios built in the low-rate era and favouring private credit, while Korea Investment Corporation called for tighter asset scrutiny and investor protection. The caution is reinforced by potential losses exceeding 220 billion won at the Korean Teachers’ Credit Union from troubled overseas property positions. Fundraising conditions are consequently tilting towards strategies offering steadier income and clearer downside control, rather than higher-beta or refinancing-sensitive exposures.
  • A Korea–Hong Kong ETF partnership targets mainland distribution. Korea Investment Management and China Universal’s Hong Kong unit disclosed on October 26 that their first jointly developed semiconductor ETF had listed on HKEX. KIM handles product planning and investment advice; CUAM HK manages the local listing and fund. The partners intend to seek ETF Connect eligibility, which would open mainland distribution, although access is not yet secured. 
  • J.P. Morgan's Asia AUM growth outpaces plan. J.P. Morgan Asset Management's Asia-Pacific assets have risen by close to a third over 12 months to roughly US$400 billion, ahead of the run-rate implied by its US$600 billion target for 2030, with private-wealth clients accounting for much of the gain. The composition of that growth — wealth-led rather than institutional-mandate-led — reflects where incremental fee pools in the region are forming, and raises the competitive bar for scaled distribution across Asian private banking channels.
  • MPF portability shifts competition toward member retention. Phase one starts on December 21 for roughly one million employees whose current jobs began on or after May 1, 2025; eligible members may transfer both employee and employer mandatory contributions to a chosen scheme once per calendar year. Giving members control over the employer-funded portion reduces the stickiness of employer-selected defaults and puts more weight on fund performance, service and member-facing distribution.
