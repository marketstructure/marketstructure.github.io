  • Sovereign Capital Realignment. Korea Investment Corporation intends to launch a new "strategic investment account" from next year, structured to hold positions in Korean semiconductor and AI companies without a fixed maturity, act as anchor capital to draw in foreign co-investors, and step in as a capital provider once finite-life state funds wind down. This moves part of Korea's sovereign toolkit away from conventional reserve-style portfolio management toward a patient-capital, market-making role. Separately, ADIA's 2025 annual review shows a material shift in its long-term strategy portfolio: the private equity target range rises to 15%–20% from 12%–17%, and financial alternatives (encompassing hedge funds) rise to 7%–12% from 5%–10%, while real estate is cut to 2%–7% from 5%–10%. For a $1 trillion allocator, this signals a structural reweighting of the fixed-income/property/private-capital mix that regional GPs raising infrastructure, buyout or credit vehicles will read as an explicit widening of the addressable sovereign pool.  
  • UBS Retreats From China Fund Distribution. UBS will shut its China fund sales unit, UBS Fund Distribution (Shenzhen), known as UBSFS, ceasing fund subscriptions and switches on its WE.UBS digital platform at the end of September, after struggling to attract investors in a market saturated with well-capitalized domestic rivals. The closure is being framed as a "business integration" rather than a market exit: affected resources are being folded into UBS's other China wealth-management platforms, which continue to operate. The episode is notable as a market-structure marker rather than an isolated retreat — it makes UBS the latest in a string of foreign institutions to concede that standalone fund-distribution platforms cannot achieve scale against deep-pocketed domestic incumbents in China's wealth market, even as the bank maintains its broader onshore wealth franchise.
  • Jane Street's Rise as an Asian Block Trader. Jane Street has emerged as one of Asia's leading block traders, moving into a segment of the market historically dominated by long/short hedge funds, following the collapse of Segantii Capital Management. Segantii wound down in 2024 after Hong Kong's Securities and Futures Commission brought insider-dealing charges against founder Simon Sadler — once dubbed Asia's "block trade king" — over a 2017 trade in Esprit Holdings shares; Sadler and the firm are currently on trial in Hong Kong. The shift matters for market structure: block trading, where banks place large off-market share sales with willing buyers, has effectively passed from a hedge fund known for balance-sheet risk-taking to a proprietary trading firm, altering who provides liquidity and absorbs risk in Asia's off-exchange equity market.
  • Private Markets: Asia Fundraising Momentum. Asian private-capital fundraising is holding up across strategies for managers who can point to deployment records or sovereign anchor backing. KKR set a new regional infrastructure record with a $6.6 billion first close on Asia Pacific Infrastructure Investors III—already ahead of the $6.4 billion final close of its predecessor—with most of the capital reportedly committed to deals before the raise is even complete. Sovereign anchoring is doing similar work further down the risk curve: Granite Asia closed its Libra Hybrid pan-Asia private credit strategy above its $500 million target on the back of Temasek, Khazanah Nasional and the Indonesia Investment Authority, adding a leading insurer and DBS Private Bank along the way, and has since put the capital to work across eight transactions and two exits. Venture is proving more idiosyncratic: China-focused 5Y Capital, an early backer of Moonshot AI, Xiaomi and Bambu Lab, is seeking $600 million for a new growth fund targeted to close in early 2027, a single-manager raise rather than evidence of a broader China venture rebound.
  • EQT's Evergreen Asia Platform. EQT has launched EQT Nexus Asia, an evergreen strategy giving eligible individual and institutional investors access to its Asia Pacific private capital platform across large-cap and mid-market strategies spanning India, Korea, Japan, Greater China, Southeast Asia and ANZ. This extends the evergreen, wealth-channel fund structure already established in EQT's global business specifically into Asian private equity distribution, widening access beyond traditional closed-end, institutional-only vehicles.

  • BlackRock's Hong Kong Tokenised Money-Market Fund. BlackRock has received regulatory approval for the BlackRock HKD Digital Liquidity Fund, its first tokenised product in Asia Pacific, and the first Hong Kong-incorporated money-market fund offering a stable NAV to both institutional and retail investors. Subscription and redemption will run through HKDAP, a regulated HKD stablecoin — placing tokenised settlement rails directly inside a mainstream cash-management product rather than a niche digital-asset wrapper, a meaningful marker for how domestic liquidity infrastructure is being redesigned in Hong Kong.

  • Taiwan's Asset-Management Hub Build-Out. President Lai Ching-te told a Taipei financial development forum that the government will intensify its Asian asset-management centre initiative and continue easing regulation to internationalize Taiwan's financial market. This policy push is now visibly converging with product innovation: AllianzGI has listed an active semiconductor-focused ETF in Taiwan, its latest entrant in a local active ETF market that exchange officials are positioning as central to the hub strategy.  
  • Sovereign Investment Council for the China-ASEAN Corridor. China Investment Corporation, Thailand's Government Pension Fund, Khazanah Nasional, KWAP (Malaysia's Retirement Fund) and Indonesia Investment Authority have formally established the China-ASEAN Joint Investment Council, announced at CIFIT in Xiamen, alongside Azerbaijan's State Oil Fund (SOFAZ) as a founding member. SOFAZ's inclusion is notable precisely because it sits outside both China and ASEAN — its presence signals the Council is being built as an open sovereign co-investment structure rather than a strictly regional bloc, extending a relationship SOFAZ already has with CIC through the bilateral Galaxy Orientis platform launched earlier this year with INA. This creates a standing coordination mechanism among six sovereign pools for corridor-specific dealflow, indicating that sovereign capital is now organizing around China-ASEAN investment themes through repeatable, multi-party structures rather than one-off vehicles.
