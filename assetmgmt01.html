  • China’s fund closures expose policy–demand mismatch. With 256 publicly offered funds liquidated and another 46 warning of possible closure, shutdowns are heading towards an eight-year high despite Beijing’s policy push to channel household savings into capital markets. The acceleration partly reflects the expiry of concessions for sponsor-backed funds encouraged during the 2023 confidence-restoration initiative: these vehicles could launch below the usual RMB200 million minimum but must terminate if assets remain below RMB50 million after three years, a provision driving nearly 100 liquidations in 2026. Weak returns and redemptions are also bringing established products below regulatory viability thresholds, while continued launches lifted the total fund universe more than 10% year-on-year to 14,585 by end-August. The combination exposes the limits of supply-led industry expansion: easier product formation and manager seed capital have not secured durable household participation, leaving an expanding fund universe alongside mounting subscale closures.
  • Cross-border manufacturing meets distribution. Nomura Asset Management is using renewed international demand for Japanese equities and bonds to attract clients through its enlarged overseas network, while building global-equity and corporate-credit capabilities; management identifies the distribution acquired with Macquarie’s US and European public-markets businesses as the platform for that expansion. Korea Investment is pursuing the complementary route of sourcing overseas capabilities for domestic wealth clients: its chairman says an overseas asset-manager acquisition is nearly complete, while its securities arm is working with AQR on fund registration and phased distribution of quantitative and alternative strategies. The common mechanism is tighter integration of investment manufacturing and client access—Nomura taking Japanese expertise into global channels, Korea Investment bringing global strategies into a domestic wealth franchise reporting monthly financial-product net inflows of about KRW2.5 trillion.
  • Korean semiconductor rally tests ETF construction. Vanguard will move its US$3.2 billion developed Asia-Pacific ex-Japan ETF to a daily-capped benchmark around 15 October, as rising Korean technology-stock weights threaten UCITS diversification limits—a compliance-driven adjustment rather than an active allocation decision. Meanwhile, Korea’s semiconductor rally is broadening into smaller materials, parts and equipment suppliers, whose specialist ETFs gained roughly 24–26% over the month to 8 October as KOSDAQ recovered. Together, the developments highlight two distinct portfolio-construction issues within the same sector: managing mega-cap concentration in broad regional benchmarks and accessing the wider semiconductor supply chain through more targeted vehicles.
  • Temasek consolidates asset-management platform. Temasek will combine Azalea Investment Management, SeaTown Holdings International and Seviora Capital under a unified Seviora operating model from January 2027, bringing private equity fund solutions, private credit, Asia-focused private equity and liquid alternatives into one client platform. The consolidation is designed to simplify investor access through a single relationship while creating greater operating scale for investment in research, technology and distribution; Fullerton Fund Management and InnoVen Capital will remain separate entities.
  • Schroders reinforces Hong Kong after Nuveen transaction. Schroders plans further investment and hiring in Hong Kong following its acquisition by Nuveen, while retaining operational autonomy for 12–18 months and cooperating across commercial distribution. Management positioned Hong Kong as its interface with mainland China through channels including Wealth Management Connect, while the combined group’s broader capabilities in wealth, retirement and private markets provide the basis for a larger regional product and distribution footprint.
  • Singapore reviews family-office asset eligibility. MAS is considering adding digital payment tokens and insurance policies to the Designated Investment list governing assets eligible for tax exemption under Singapore’s fund-incentive schemes. The review responds to single-family offices seeking greater portfolio flexibility and would broaden the range of wealth-planning and investment structures that can sit within incentivised vehicles; MAS has not yet confirmed the final inclusions or implementation timetable.
