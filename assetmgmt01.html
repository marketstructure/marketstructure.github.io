  • China-linked wealth enters a compliance phase. The latest read-through from Hong Kong and Singapore is that Beijing’s offshore-trust and insurance taxation is reshaping the servicing model rather than yet producing a verified exodus of private wealth.   • Hong Kong draws the line at client capital. Proposed amendments to Hong Kong’s fund-tax regime would materially widen the 0% treatment of eligible carried interest and performance fees beyond private equity, encompassing qualifying fund strategies across public markets, private credit, real estate and virtual assets; they would also expand the types of structures that may qualify, including certain pension, endowment and single-investor funds. The government has now ruled out an important extension: proprietary trading firms cannot access the concession because they generate returns on their own account, rather than from managing pooled or client capital. The practical signal is that the tax package is designed to compete for investment-management mandates, decision-makers and fund infrastructure—not to subsidise balance-sheet trading.
