  • Mainland insurance capital into Hong Kong ETFs. Currently 31 Hong Kong ETFs with market capitalisation of about HK$343.6 billion are eligible under Stock Connect, giving insurers immediate access to a diversified product set and reinforcing Hong Kong’s role as a cross‑border fund distribution hub.

  • Fidelity weighs China fund-management exit. Fidelity International is considering a full exit from its wholly owned Shanghai fund-management subsidiary, only three years after launch, subject to final internal deliberation and regulatory approval. The unit’s 14 retail funds held RMB4.5 billion (US$670 million) at end-June—25% below their RMB6 billion peak and far short of Fidelity’s internal estimate of at least US$14 billion needed for profitability by 2029. Fierce domestic competition, recurring leadership turnover and failure to establish scale have made the onshore retail model untenable, despite Fidelity managing US$1.18 trillion globally; the prospective withdrawal would rank among the largest retrenchments by an international manager from China’s mutual-fund market.

  • Korea mobilises patient public capital for strategic technology and private equity. Complementing this, NH Investment & Securities is exclusively distributing the “Hyundai Investment Select Mixed Assets Investment Trust No.1”, a public‑type private equity fund‑of‑funds that gives retail investors diversified access to multiple private equity funds, broadening Korea’s household participation in private markets through regulated structures.

Malaysia’s EPF reported RM29.77 billion of second-quarter investment income, up 44% year on year, taking first-half income to RM57.5 billion; international assets, 39% of its portfolio, generated 65% of quarterly investment income.

  • Chinese wealth structures tested by offshore trust tax clampdown. New rules unveiled in late July impose a 20% individual income tax on gains when assets such as shares or property are transferred into offshore trusts, along with an annual 20% tax on trust income and proceeds, with retroactive application to assets placed since January 2023 and a 90‑day window to report and settle unpaid liabilities. The changes, which effectively levy tax at multiple stages of a trust’s life, are forcing wealthy Chinese families to revisit long‑standing offshore trust and insurance structures and reassess jurisdictional choices for asset protection and succession planning. Against this backdrop, HSBC Private Banking reports that Greater China net new money nearly doubled in the first half of 2026, with corporate‑to‑private collaborations up 30%, illustrating how onshore corporates and private banking platforms—particularly in Hong Kong—are becoming key channels for restructuring wealth under tighter tax and regulatory oversight.

  • PAG tests appetite for a rebalanced Asian buyout strategy. PAG has begun sounding out limited partners for a new Asia buyout fund of approximately US$4–5 billion, targeting a first close by year-end. The proposed vehicle follows a US$4 billion 2024 predecessor that was invested broadly evenly across Australia, China, Japan and India, reflecting PAG’s effort to reduce its historical concentration in China. The fundraising will test whether cash distributions can translate into renewed commitments: PAG has returned more than US$7 billion to investors over the past 18 months, predominantly from China exits, after its prior fund closed materially below its original US$9 billion target amid China’s regulatory tightening and geopolitical pressure on North American LP appetite.
