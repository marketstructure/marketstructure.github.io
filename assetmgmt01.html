  • South Korea's mandatory trading course curbs leveraged chip-ETF speculation. Trading value in leveraged ETFs tracking twice the daily returns of Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix has collapsed to roughly 4% of its June peak, putting the products on track for their first monthly outflow, after regulators introduced an onerous mock-trading qualification course aimed at cooling retail speculation . The episode illustrates how a targeted product-access restriction, rather than an outright product ban, can rapidly reprice risk appetite in a market — Korea's US$4.3 trillion equity market — that had become unusually volatile on leveraged single-theme exposure.
  • ExodusPoint discloses scale of its Asia build-out following the Ovata absorption. The nearly US$15 billion multistrategy manager is on track to grow its Hong Kong, Singapore and Tokyo headcount to roughly 100 by year-end, up almost 80% from 56 at the start of 2026, having already secured more than 20,000 square feet of office space across the three cities with capacity for up to 180 staff . The figures quantify, for the first time, the extent to which absorbing Ovata Capital's roughly 40-person Hong Kong equities team is driving a step-change in ExodusPoint's regional platform rather than a routine hire.

The US trading firm is scouting more than 50,000 square feet at Cheung Kong Center II — roughly triple its current footprint — to support a hiring push aimed at deepening its market-making access to China-linked ETFs, according to sources cited by Reuters. The move lands against a backdrop of record Hong Kong ETF turnover, which hit HK$63.5 billion a day in June on a market that has grown its asset base at a compound annual rate of over 20% over five years, making a larger liquidity-provision presence in the city a direct bet on that structural volume growth continuing.
  • Hong Kong's SFC tightens private-market fund classification, raising the retail distribution bar. The regulator will reclassify authorised funds with 50% or more net asset value exposure to private credit or equity—including indirect exposure via layered structures—as complex products, subjecting them to enhanced suitability and disclosure requirements before retail sale. The move directly addresses transparency gaps in structures used to package illiquid private-market exposure for retail distribution, tightening the channel through which such products reach Hong Kong's mass-affluent investor base.
  • China's NSSF expands the overseas pool competing managers are chasing. The National Council for Social Security Fund reported a 13.22% investment return for 2025 — nearly double the prior year's 8.1% — and lifted its overseas allocation to 15.23% of assets, equivalent to roughly RMB 580 billion, up from 13.13% a year earlier. NSSF has historically routed its offshore book through a roster of appointed external managers rather than running it in-house, so a rising overseas share directly expands the capital base available to that external mandate structure, even as the fund's own commentary keeps its domestic equity stance framed around long-cycle, patient-capital positioning rather than a shift away from its home market.
  • CFA Institute survey quantifies the scale of mainland Chinese capital seeking offshore diversification. Among 300 affluent Chinese investors aged 18-44 surveyed in December, 81% reported holding offshore assets, averaging 43% of portfolios, rising to 93% and 45% respectively among those with international study, work or business experience. The finding indicates offshore allocation has become a mainstream behavior among China's younger wealthy cohort rather than a niche hedge, despite prevailing capital controls, with direct implications for the private banks and platforms competing to capture that outbound flow. 
  • AllianzGI's UOB Asset Management deal to reshape Southeast Asian distribution economics. The roughly €376 million acquisition, agreed last month, gives AllianzGI an on-the-ground licensed presence across Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia and Vietnam, paired with a long-term distribution agreement granting access to UOB's 8 million-plus retail clients across some 400 branches, lifting AllianzGI's regional client assets above €170 billion. The structure — combining an asset-management acquisition with a bank-distribution tie-up — remains a template for foreign managers seeking scaled retail access in Southeast Asia without building branch networks organically.
  • Federated Hermes enters tokenized fund distribution in Asia-Pacific via Conduit Digital alliance. Under the arrangement, a fund managed by Singapore's Conduit Digital Holdings will invest in Federated Hermes' US$676.9 billion-backed Short-Term U.S. Prime Fund, with Conduit fund shares then tokenized for distribution to institutional and wholesale investors across the region — the manager's first digital-assets initiative in Asia-Pacific . The deal follows Federated Hermes' earlier tokenized UCITS money-market structure on the Archax exchange and lands within days of Mirae Asset, Citi and OSL launching Hong Kong's first tokenized covered-call ETF, pointing to a broadening set of global and regional managers using tokenized wrappers to distribute existing fund exposures into Asia-Pacific rather than building novel underlying products.
  • Sustainable Finance Initiative survey shows Asia-Pacific family offices concentrating impact allocations rather than expanding them. Polling 121 family office representatives and asset owners, the Hong Kong-based SFi found 86% now hold some sustainable-investment exposure — flat versus prior years — but the share allocating more than half their portfolio to impact and ESG strategies jumped to 27% from 17% in 2025, while those exceeding a 10% impact threshold rose to 58%. Reliance on simple portfolio carve-outs fell to 13% from 18%, with systems-level and total-portfolio approaches overtaking opportunistic allocation as the dominant methodology, and food/agriculture and water-sanitation-hygiene themes displacing broader ESG categories as top thematic priorities. The pattern indicates conviction is deepening among an already-committed base of allocators rather than impact investing broadening to new adopters.
