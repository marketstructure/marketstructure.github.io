  • China fund profits and quant resilience. China’s mutual fund firms reported first-half net profit of 8.9 billion yuan across 24 companies, up 47.4% year-on-year, with the four most profitable managers accounting for more than half of the total, underscoring both earnings recovery and a sharply polarized industry structure. Meanwhile, quantitative hedge funds – which suffered steep losses in July’s AI-linked rout – have since seen their most popular strategies outperform domestic benchmarks, signalling that systematic products remain central to China’s onshore risk-taking despite recent drawdowns.

  • Hedge funds: Japan expansion meets AI de-risking. Global hedge funds are deepening their commitment to Japan even as they pare back crowded Asian technology exposures. Marshall Wace, the US$90bn UK manager, plans to open a small Tokyo investment office in 2027, subject to regulatory approval, joining peers expanding local research and investment capabilities. At the same time, Morgan Stanley prime-brokerage data show that hedge funds made Asia-Pacific the largest component of their second-biggest weekly global equity sell-off in 12 months, cutting semiconductors, electronic equipment and hardware across Japan, China, Korea and Taiwan. Net exposure to Japan, Korea and Taiwan fell to about 17% of global allocations from around 23% in late June. The retrenchment follows July's sharp AI-led correction, yet Asian family offices are reportedly returning to liquid hedge funds for downside protection while retaining selective exposure further down the AI supply chain — a sign that allocators are rotating from broad beta-like technology risk towards liquidity, hedging and more discriminating AI opportunities.

  • Japan–Vietnam asset management corridor. Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Group is preparing to enter Vietnam’s asset management market via a joint venture with state-owned BIDV, with a 49/51 ownership split and launch targeted as early as next year, aiming to capture rising household financial assets and demand for professionally managed funds. In parallel, Amova Asset Management and BIDV Securities have agreed to establish a domestic asset management JV, signalling that Vietnam’s fast-growing capital market is becoming a structured entry point for Japanese managers seeking regional growth and local distribution partners. 

  • ETF innovation, AI exposure and tokenization. China's regulators have approved the first batch of 18 active ETFs, opening a new segment in a market previously dominated by passive beta and creating a listed-format avenue for alpha-seeking strategies. Korea shows how ETF wrappers are being stretched to blend local equity exposure with fixed income and retirement channels. Samsung Asset Management's KODEX Korea TDF 2065 Active ETF allocates 100% to domestic stocks and bonds with life-cycle rebalancing. Mirae Asset's TIGER Samsung Electronics SK Hynix US Treasury Mixed 50 ETF pairs the two leading chipmakers with 0–1 year US Treasuries, and qualifies for full retirement-pension allocation. In Hong Kong, Global X, Citi and OSL have introduced a tokenized unit class for the Global X HSCEI Covered Call Active ETF — the first covered-call ETF in the market to offer tokenized shares on a regulated digital-asset platform. The structure bridges traditional equity-income strategies with blockchain-based custody and could widen distribution among digitally native investors.

  • Private credit and private equity capital formation. Temasek-backed Azalea Investment Management has raised more than US$1 billion in new capital commitments across three private equity offerings, including a closed-end flagship fund, a co-investment vehicle and a debut evergreen fund, indicating persistent investor appetite for diversified access structures even in a “challenging” PE environment. Granite Asia has exceeded its US$500 million target for the pan-Asia Libra Hybrid private credit fund, with anchor commitments from Khazanah, Indonesia Investment Authority and Temasek, plus new capital from a leading insurer and DBS Private Bank clients, underlining the growing role of sovereigns and private banks in scaling regional private credit. Indonesia’s sovereign wealth fund Danantara has awarded a US$1 billion private credit mandate to Partners Group—US$600 million for direct lending and US$400 million in discretionary and co-invest tranches. 

  • Digital assets strategies and wealth-management competition. Mirae Asset’s chairman has set an explicit target of KRW150 trillion (around US$100 billion) in digital assets—roughly 10% of the group’s client assets—built around crypto, stablecoins, real-world assets and security tokens via the Digital X platform, signalling an institutional-scale commitment to tokenized finance with a profitability goal by 2027. HashKey Exchange’s partnership with Franklin Templeton to distribute the US$720 million Franklin OnChain U.S. Government Liquidity Fund to professional investors in Asia via a licensed Earn channel further embeds tokenized money-market products into the regional wealth architecture, while keeping access restricted to qualified clients under Hong Kong rules. 

  • Asia's $10tn wealth transfer. More than US$10tn of personal wealth in Asia is expected to pass between generations over the next 20–25 years, yet local wealth managers are losing the race to capture it, according to a new Deloitte report. The prize is substantial: HNW and investable assets in Asia reached US$27tn in 2024, second only to North America, and the region held US$169.7tn in total personal wealth in 2025 — roughly a third of the global total. Despite this scale, Asia Pacific–headquartered firms continue to cede the league tables to global brands, even as the next generation of clients brings different investment horizons, risk appetites and service preferences. Deloitte's warning to incumbents: advantages in trust, funding and risk may help retain customers, "but loyalty cannot be assumed."
