  • Mainland outbound channels broaden. BlackRock’s wholly owned China fund-management unit received QDII status, becoming the fourth wholly foreign-owned mutual-fund manager authorised to invest domestic capital overseas. Separately, mainland insurers have begun investing in Hong Kong-listed ETFs after gaining access through Stock Connect in August, with higher ETF turnover interpreted as evidence of initial deployment. Together, the developments expand regulated outbound access through two distinct institutional channels: fund products serving onshore investors and insurer balance sheets entering Hong Kong’s ETF market.
  • Asia-Pacific family offices favour direct investment. Citi’s 2026 survey found that 26% of Asia-Pacific family offices reported year-to-date portfolio returns above 15%, more than twice the global share, while 80% identified AI as a primary focus for direct investment. The combination of strong reported returns and interest in investing directly in AI businesses puts deal sourcing, access and due diligence at the centre of the proposition for managers serving these families.

  • Taiwan advances its hub strategy. Taiwan’s long-delayed asset-management-centre initiative is moving from policy design into implementation, with the Kaohsiung pilot zone attracting more than NT$728 billion, or US$22.8 billion, during its first year. The programme combines wealth-management experimentation with planned regulatory liberalisation and broader cross-border financial services, giving Taiwan a more concrete platform for retaining domestic wealth and attracting international managers rather than relying principally on its deep pool of household savings.

  • Korean pension capital targets India. South Korea’s National Pension Service is seeking approval to invest in Indian government securities through a streamlined registration route established by India’s market regulator for foreign investors confined to sovereign bonds. As one of the first major pension funds to use the lower-compliance pathway, NPS would provide an institutional test of India’s efforts to convert regulatory simplification into stable foreign demand for its government-debt market.

  • Korea mobilises retail growth capital. The Financial Services Commission will seek 600 billion won from individuals for a second five-year public-participation growth fund, matching the first vehicle, which sold out early. Capital will be allocated across ten sub-funds, while government and manager seed money will absorb the first 18.8%–23.3% of losses at sub-fund level; half the offering is initially reserved for lower-income investors, supported by tax deductions and distribution through ten banks and 14 securities firms. The structure combines public risk-sharing, tax incentives and mass-market distribution to channel household savings into higher-risk growth investments.

  • Co-investment fundraising scales up. Schroders Capital closed Global Direct IV at US$1.3 billion, its largest closed-ended commingled private-equity fund, targeting mainly European and US businesses with enterprise values below US$1 billion. The record close demonstrates institutional capacity for diversified co-investment vehicles that offer access alongside specialist general partners while concentrating on the small- and mid-market segment.
