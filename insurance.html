This briefing is generated by an AI-augmented workflow with some light review. Please verify critical information independently.

  • China life insurers post strong H1 earnings ahead of Q3 reporting. Ping An reported first-half net profit rose 36.1 percent year over year to RMB 92.585 billion, with life and health insurance operating profit increasing 2.3 percent to RMB 55.9 billion, while asset management net profit surged 209.4 percent. PICC Group similarly posted H1 2026 net profit of RMB 50,836 million, up 40.6 percent year-over-year, with insurance revenue rising 2.4 percent to RMB 286,873 million, driven partly by total investment income climbing 62.7 percent to RMB 66,327 million. 

  • Bancassurance commission reforms bite AIA in China. AIA's China bancassurance sales fell 6% in H1 even as the overall market grew, costing it substantial market share, per Jefferies. Commission-disclosure reforms that took effect in July appear to have pulled demand forward — sales surged in Q2 before falling materially — and Jefferies expects a weak Q3. Bancassurance accounts for roughly 15% of AIA China's VONB (itself 29% of group VONB in H1), implying a potential ~2.1% group-level drag, though it expects share to recover from 2027 as competitors adjust.

  • PICC P&C returns to Hong Kong's cat bond market for a second Great Wall Re issuance. PICC P&C appears to have returned to sponsor its second catastrophe bond using its Hong Kong domiciled special purpose insurer Great Wall Re Limited, with a tranche of floating rate notes pricing on September 30th and maturing in roughly one year. The new issuance is reportedly sized at $12 million, smaller than its 2022 debut deal, underscoring continued use of Hong Kong's ILS regime by mainland insurers for catastrophe risk transfer.

  • India's IRDAI distribution overhaul triggers industry-wide pushback. The regulator's plan to cap commissions and cut expense-of-management limits is facing resistance, with the Insurance Brokers Association of India warning the proposals could put at least 1 million livelihoods at risk over a five-year period and could lower broker revenues by nearly 70%. Brokers are seeking an extension of the consultation deadline to late December from October 25, an independent impact assessment, and removal of the commission caps, while separately, survey data shows brokers arguing that lower commissions may not necessarily translate into cheaper premiums for policyholders even as most buyers surveyed back the caps.

  • Korean insurers reshuffle financial-services portfolios. Hanwha Life is acquiring a 50.54% controlling stake in Acuon Capital from EQT for KRW440bn (~$325m) in a co-investment with Centroid, securing both the finance company and its wholly owned Acuon Savings Bank; it plans to merge the latter with Hanwha Savings Bank to create a ~KRW6.5trn savings banking group. Meritz, which lost out in that process, is reportedly moving to sell its 51% stake in its Indonesian JV. Samsung's insurance affiliates also joined the $1bn Helix investment.

  • Japan sees major reinsurance and corporate restructuring activity. Prismic Life is set to reinsure $5bn of Prudential's Japanese whole life policies, while Nippon Life stands to benefit from affiliate Corebridge's merger with Equitable—both signaling continued cross-border consolidation and block-reinsurance activity involving Japanese life insurers' overseas affiliates.

  • MAS finalizes AI risk guidelines for financial institutions. Singapore's central bank issued Guidelines on AI Risk Management on 7 October, applying to all financial institutions and all forms of AI. The guidelines set supervisory expectations for managing AI-related risks while allowing firms to tailor approaches to their scale of AI use, with phased implementation beginning October 2027.
