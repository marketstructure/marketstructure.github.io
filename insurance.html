  • Hong Kong: product launches and coverage expansion. AIA launched a lifetime annuity product aimed at the territory's retirement protection gap, while FWD rolled out its new "Crisis AllSupporter Insurance Plan" series and HSBC Life deepened its cross-border healthcare proposition with four new lounges. Coverage is also being extended administratively, with mandatory insurance broadened without additional premiums, including an extension to Huanggang Port. These launches come as the Hong Kong life market is projected to expand 5–10%.

  • FWD takes 25% stake in Bolttech Insurance (Hong Kong) in connected-party deal. FWD Group announced that its subsidiary FWD Limited has agreed to acquire a 25% non-controlling stake in Bolttech Insurance (Hong Kong) from Bolttech Discovery Holdings for a base consideration of US$20.72m (HK$161.62m), subject to a closing adjustment referenced to the target's net asset value, solvency ratio and combined ratio at the latest fiscal quarter before completion. Bolttech Insurance (Hong Kong) underwrites general insurance in and from Hong Kong and is ultimately owned by bolttech Holdings, in which FWD controlling shareholder Richard Li indirectly holds 30% or more. 

  • Korea: Shinhan's Lotte Non-Life acquisition and Samsung Life's offshore capital raise. Shinhan Financial Group's board approved the acquisition of a 77.04% stake in Lotte Non-Life Insurance, Korea's seventh-largest non-life insurer, from JKL Partners for KRW693.3bn (~US$670m+), with plans to move to 100% ownership after regulatory approval and merge the business with Shinhan EZ General Insurance—a deal framed as a bid to close the non-life gap with KB. The surprise agreement followed exclusive talks that had collapsed in July. Separately, Samsung Life disclosed plans to raise up to US$1.5bn via 30-year hybrid capital securities offshore before year-end to bolster K-ICS solvency, while confirming it is exploring global expansion; the size mirrors the reported ~US$1.47bn price tag for a Canopius stake.

  • India: premium momentum and market development. Life insurance premiums rose 21.3%, with private insurers outpacing LIC, and ICICI Life's Q2 net profit climbed 25% on a better product mix. Insurtech ACKO is preparing to go public, and a pair of shipbuilding credit risk insurance policies was launched — the latest in a run of policy initiatives supporting domestic industry.

  • S&P flags data centre accumulation risk for Asia-Pacific insurers. In an 8 October briefing following its Asia-Pacific Insurance Virtual Conference, S&P Global Ratings warned that rapid AI infrastructure build-out is creating exposures that increasingly extend beyond traditional insurance categories, with data centres a particular concern. Their reliance on shared, interconnected power systems, contractors and technology networks makes it difficult to trace where a loss originates and how it could propagate across multiple assets and policies—complicating business-interruption cover and accumulation modelling. S&P noted insurers are under pressure to make underwriting decisions more quickly despite limited historical loss data and growing uncertainty over how emerging risks interact, raising the prospect that a single event could hit several lines (property, BI, cyber, liability) and several insurers' portfolios simultaneously.

  • Generali–World Bank insurance partnership. An MoU signed on 13 October establishes cooperation on insurance solutions initially focused on Asian markets, with three priorities: climate and crop insurance, disaster protection, and coverage for ageing populations. The parties also agreed to support diversified disaster-risk pooling structures and climate-related pooling mechanisms across emerging regions. A separate investment component covers creating investable opportunities to channel institutional capital towards frontier and emerging markets; the announcement did not specify an investment allocation or country-level implementation programme.
  • Reinsurance capital transactions. Details reported on 14 October identified PICC P&C’s US$12m Great Wall Re issuance as Hong Kong’s first ILS sidecar, providing quota-share protection for mainland offshore-windfarm property insurance. Pacific Life Re announced on 13 October its second Japanese structured-reinsurance transaction of 2026, with an unnamed leading life insurer. The deal is designed to improve the cedant’s capital efficiency and optimise its economic capital position under Japan’s Economic Solvency Ratio framework.
