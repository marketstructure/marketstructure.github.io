  • China: insurer asset-allocation opening; early effects of tighter wealth and distribution controls. The NFRA’s August 18 decision enables mainland insurers to invest in Hong Kong-listed ETFs through Southbound Stock Connect, opening a new permitted channel for insurers’ offshore asset allocation through Hong Kong; 31 ETFs, with aggregate market capitalisation of HK$343.6 billion, are currently eligible under the programme. Separately, Fitch reported this week that life premium growth slowed to 3.6% year-on-year in H1 2026, with July’s bancassurance commission-expense rules expected to restrain near-term sales as carriers adapt distribution economics. Reuters also reported a fresh practical consequence of Beijing’s late-July offshore-trust tax overhaul: tax authorities in major cities have begun enforcing tax on returns from offshore insurance policies, alongside the new 20% taxation of trust asset transfers and annual trust income. This has prompted some wealthy clients to reassess liabilities, funding needs and existing offshore trust structures, including insurance-linked holdings.

  • Hong Kong’s life and health market: regulatory continuity, balance sheet commitment and product/payment innovation. Hong Kong’s Insurance Authority has reappointed Clement Cheung as CEO for another three-year term from 15 August 2026 to 14 August 2029, reinforcing leadership continuity as the market navigates mainland tax changes and cross‑border capital oversight. AIA reported an 11% rise in first-half 2026 operating profit (and a double‑digit increase in value of new business) driven by strong Hong Kong, mainland China and broader Asian sales, underscoring the territory’s continuing role as its largest and most profitable market. Prudential Hong Kong has agreed to expand and upgrade its headquarters footprint across Swire Properties’ Taikoo Place towers, increasing its office space to roughly 83,000 square feet, signalling a long-term commitment to the city even as Beijing tightens wealth oversight. On the product side, Chubb Life Hong Kong has launched the Chubb Care Critical Illness Series, covering 146 conditions with flexible payouts and unified premium scales for customers in Hong Kong and mainland China, while Bowtie’s new “StillCover” supplementary medical plan targets chronic disease patients using co‑insurance and benefits structured around public hospital usage and self-financed drugs. Meanwhile, HashKey Exchange and YF Life completed Hong Kong’s first live insurance payment test using HKDAP, the city’s first regulated HKD‑backed stablecoin, validating subscription and redemption flows and paving the way for potential stablecoin-based premium payments once regulatory approvals are in place.

  • South Korea’s insurance M&A cycle reactivates amid capital-structure moves. Woori Financial Group has announced the merger of Tongyang Life Insurance and ABL Life Insurance into a single life insurer with around 55 trillion won in assets, targeting a launch in the second half of 2027 as part of a strategy to build scale and embed life operations more deeply within the banking group. Asia Insurance Review notes that South Korea’s broader insurance M&A market is “poised for revival,” with processes for KDB Life, Lotte Insurance, BNP Paribas Cardif Life and AXA General seen moving toward key decision points. In that context, JKL Partners’ sale of Lotte Non‑Life Insurance to Shinhan Financial collapsed after a mezzanine investor blocked the transaction over valuation, prompting JKL to pivot to an open auction format for the asset. Separately, Kyobo Securities has resolved to underwrite 40 billion won of 30‑year hybrid capital securities being issued by affiliate Kyobo Life as part of a larger 300 billion won (with potential upsize) capital transaction, further evidence of active balance‑sheet management in the sector.

  • India clears multiple strategic investments in life and insurtech while drafting nuclear liability insurance rules. A Boston Consulting Group report finds that the country’s general insurance industry grew 9% in FY26, with gross direct premium income reaching about ₹3.36 trillion, driven particularly by health and motor lines. On the corporate side, the Competition Commission of India (CCI) has approved Prudential Corporation Holdings’ ₹3,500 crore acquisition of a 75% stake in Bharti Life, contingent on Prudential cutting its holding in ICICI Prudential Life below 10%, effectively reshaping its Indian life footprint. CCI has also cleared BNP Paribas Cardif’s return to India as a strategic investor via the acquisition of roughly 26% of IndiaFirst Life from Warburg Pincus, and approved General Atlantic’s plan to lift its stake in digital general insurer Acko above the 25% threshold through a rights issue. Separately, draft SHANTI Rules released by the government mandate that nuclear plant operators maintain insurance or other financial security for nuclear damage, decommissioning and waste management, with operator liability set on a strict, no‑fault basis and linked to installation size, and with financial security required to remain in force until spent fuel is fully removed from storage.

  • Singapore and Thailand move to tighten claims transparency and dispute handling. MSIG Singapore has released what it describes as the first standalone claims transparency report by a general insurer in the market, disclosing that it paid S$132.4 million in claims in 2025 at a 94% settlement ratio and highlighting automation that has cut turnaround times on some travel claims by roughly two‑thirds. In Thailand, the Office of the Insurance Commission has convened insurers and regional offices and set out a series of measures to improve complaint and claims management, including new KPIs for claims handling, enhanced screening by complaint units before escalation to the OIC, streamlined claims approval authorities, a requirement that insurers review all cases submitted through the Insurance Service Center, inspections of the five insurers with the highest complaint ratios, and expanded regional oversight of market conduct.
