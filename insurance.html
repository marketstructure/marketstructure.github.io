  • Mainland China: insurer capital allocation and digital insurance platforms. China Life Asset Management has set out a more explicit framing for insurers’ role in technology financing: vice-president Fan Yanming said the emphasis is shifting from expanding the amount of insurance money invested in technology toward improving the quality of deployment, using insurers’ long-dated and stable funding base to support harder-to-finance innovation. Separately, Waterdrop reported Q2 revenue of RMB1.45 billion, up 72.8% year-on-year, with insurance revenue up 80.5% to RMB1.33 billion. The company attributed growth to insurance-business expansion and AI-enabled operations, and said AI-supported customer engagement generated nearly RMB100 million in first-year premiums during the quarter.

  • Hong Kong: insurance authorities and insurers advance distinct risk-advisory and AI agendas. The Insurance Authority is positioning Hong Kong as a one-stop risk-advisory and risk-management hub for mainland companies expanding overseas, particularly as Belt and Road-related activity entails more complex global operations. Separately, AXA Hong Kong and Macau has entered an MoU with BytePlus to introduce AI and LLM applications across its operations, including underwriting, distribution and claims-knowledge management. The initiative follows a wider shift toward using AI tools to give advisers faster preliminary risk assessments at the point of sale.

  • South Korea: solvency metrics weaken modestly as consolidation and automation reshape life insurance. Aggregate insurers’ K-ICS ratio declined 0.8 percentage points quarter-on-quarter to 215.2% at end-June, amid equity-market volatility and associated capital requirements; available capital was reported at KRW380 trillion. In life insurance, Kyobo Life has approved the absorption of its wholly owned internet insurer, Kyobo Lifeplanet—South Korea’s first internet-only life insurer, launched in 2013—bringing its business and personnel into the parent after sustained standalone profitability and capital challenges.  Separately, the National Pension Service has signed an agreement with LG AI Research to develop finance-specific AI tools for fund management, with AI-based foreign-exchange analysis identified as the first joint project. The proposed model will combine macroeconomic indicators, news and research material with NPS data through LG’s EXAONE Business Intelligence platform.  

  • Southeast Asia: discrete developments in cyber insurance, public agricultural-risk support, and reinsurance broking. Singapore cyber-insurance pricing has eased as cover has widened: starting premiums for S$2 million of coverage for mid-market companies have fallen 25%, from S$8,000 in 2022 to S$6,000, while S$250,000 limits are now available from S$1,000 annually for some technology and financial-sector buyers. Vietnam has increased agricultural-insurance premium support through Decree 346/2026/ND-CP: subsidies can reach 95% for poor or near-poor households, 50% for other individuals and 30% for qualifying agricultural organizations; the framework also broadens eligible agricultural exposures. In Malaysia, Miller has opened a Labuan-licensed operation focused initially on treaty and facultative reinsurance.

  • India: conduct enforcement and tax exposure at major life insurers. IRDAI has imposed a ₹1 crore penalty on Canara HSBC Life over the sale of a deferred-annuity policy to an 88-year-old customer. The regulator identified failures in product suitability, solicitation, disclosure and internal controls; reporting indicates that the insurer refunded the customer’s premium and was required to review relevant policies sold to senior customers through its corporate agent, Canara Bank. Separately, HDFC Life has received an appeal order confirming a GST demand and penalty of approximately ₹3,365 crore. The company intends to pursue further legal challenge and has stated that it does not expect the order to have a material adverse effect on its financial operations.

  • Japan: Prudential Life discloses customer-data loss involving a former sales employee. Prudential Life Insurance’s Japanese operation disclosed that records affecting 1,570 customers were leaked after a former sales employee improperly removed documents from company premises, retained them after leaving the firm in March, and subsequently lost them. The information reportedly included customers’ phone numbers, addresses, employers and insurance-contract details.
